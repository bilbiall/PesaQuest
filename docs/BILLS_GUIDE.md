# 🧾 The Bills Guide — Why Pesa City Never Lets You Forget You're an Adult

*A player's guide to bills, obligations, and why your credit score actually cares.*

---

## The one-sentence version

**Nobody in Pesa City pays your bills for you — not even the game.** Money doesn't
vanish from your wallet automatically. Every bill sits there, patiently, until *you*
walk into Life HQ and pay it. That single design choice is the whole lesson: **income
feels good, obligations feel like work, and real financial skill is remembering to do
the work.**

---

## 1. What even IS a bill in this game?

Think of a bill as a recurring promise you made to someone — a landlord, an insurer, a
county government, an electricity company — that keeps coming back on a schedule,
whether you feel like paying it or not. In Pesa City, every bill has:

| Thing | What it means to you |
|---|---|
| **Amount (Ksh)** | What it costs you each time it's due |
| **Frequency** | How often it repeats — weekly, monthly, termly, or yearly |
| **Due date (in game days)** | The exact game day it's expecting payment |
| **A story** | Every bill has flavor text and a "why this matters" explanation — bills in Pesa City are never just numbers |

You'll find your bills waiting for you on your **Dashboard's Bills panel** and in full
detail at **Life HQ**. Nothing about a bill changes until you act on it.

---

## 2. Bills grow up WITH you — the Chapter system

Here's the part that used to be missing and is now the heart of how bills work: **your
bills are not all dumped on you on day one.** They arrive in step with your **Life
Chapter** — the six-stage story arc every player climbs (🎒 Student → 🎓 Graduate → 💪
Hustler → 🏠 Settler → 📊 Builder → 🌟 Elder), which itself is driven by your net worth.

Picture it like this:

> **🎒 As a Student**, your bills are pocket-money-sized — a data bundle, transport fare,
> maybe a small school-related cost. Nothing that should ever break you.
>
> **🎓 The moment you graduate**, new bills quietly show up that make sense for someone
> starting from zero — your first taste of rent, your own airtime, no parent covering the
> gap anymore.
>
> **💪 As a Hustler**, obligations grow again — you're earning more, so life expects more
> from you too.
>
> **🏠 By the time you're a Settler**, mortgage-adjacent costs and family-sized bills
> appear — the game trusts you can handle them because your net worth says you can.
>
> **📊 🌟 Builder and Elder** bring the largest, most "grown-up" obligations — the kind
> that come with real wealth and real responsibility.

You'll get a notification the moment a new bill arrives — it always explains itself
("Life as a Settler comes with new responsibilities"), so it's never a mystery. This
means two players at completely different net worths can have completely different
bill lists, and that's intentional: **your obligations should always match your stage
of life, not your character's age or a coin flip.**

---

## 3. How a bill actually reaches you

A bill can appear in one of two ways:

1. **Automatically, by chapter** — the moment your net worth crosses into a new chapter,
   any bill templates set to unlock at that chapter (or earlier) attach to your account
   automatically, matched to your age group too.
2. **Because you bought something** — some assets come with their own bills attached.
   Buy a car, and Vehicle Insurance + Fuel & Servicing show up. Buy property, and a
   Service Charge shows up. **Ownership has ongoing costs — that's the whole point of
   the lesson.** You can't buy a car in Pesa City and pretend it's free to own.

---

## 4. Paying a bill (the only way it gets paid)

Head to **Life HQ**. Every active bill shows:
- How many game days until it's due (or how overdue it is)
- The exact amount
- A **Pay Now** button

Click Pay Now, and if you have the balance, it's deducted immediately and your credit
score gets a small boost. That's it. There's no auto-pay toggle, no "set and forget."
**You are the direct cause of every bill being handled — which means you're also the
direct cause when one isn't.**

---

## 5. What happens if you don't pay (and why it's not the end of the world)

Missing a bill is realistic, not catastrophic:

- The bill flips to **Overdue** and stays on your list — it doesn't disappear or reset.
- Your credit score takes a hit (the exact size depends on the bill — essential bills
  like rent hit harder than a small subscription).
- If a bill goes multiple cycles unpaid while you're away, the credit damage **caps at
  two hits per bill per visit** — a long absence stings, but it won't wipe out your
  score in one sitting.
- Paying an overdue bill still repairs some credit — just **less** than paying on time.
  Being late is always worse than being on time, even if you eventually pay. That
  small difference is deliberate: *"better late than never" is true, but "on time" is
  still better.*

---

## 6. The credit score connection, spelled out

| Behaviour | Effect |
|---|---|
| Pay a bill on time | Credit score **up** (full amount) |
| Pay a bill that went overdue | Credit score **up** (half the usual amount — repair, not reward) |
| Miss a bill entirely | Credit score **down** |
| Miss the same bill repeatedly while away | Capped at 2 hits per visit — absence isn't a trap |

Your credit score isn't cosmetic — it's read by the loan and asset-financing systems
elsewhere in the game. A habit of ignoring bills quietly closes doors on borrowing later.
A habit of paying on time quietly opens them.

---

## 7. Why bills exist at all — the honest pitch

It would be easy to make a game where money only ever goes up. Pesa City doesn't, on
purpose. Every bill is a rehearsal for a specific real skill: **noticing an obligation
before it becomes a crisis, and choosing to deal with it instead of avoiding it.** The
size of the numbers is fictional. The habit you build clicking "Pay Now" — instead of
letting something slide — is not.
